# D6 — Human evaluation of explanation formats: study protocol

**Status:** protocol and materials ready. **No participant data has been
collected.** Nothing in this repository simulates or invents participant
responses. Results are reported as "not yet collected" until real sessions
have taken place.
**Design registered:** Study D addendum, `reports/analysis_plan_preregistration.md`
(commit c0a3ee1). This document expands that design. It does not change it.

---

## 1. Question

Which explanation format best helps CSO security engineers to:

- understand a model's vulnerability ranking;
- predict what the model will do;
- calibrate their trust in it;
- decide quickly?

This answers **SQ2** with people, complementing the technical answers from D1–D5.

## 2. Prerequisites (must be in place before the first session)

1. Ethics approval from the Maastricht University DACS ethics review committee
   (minimal-risk study with expert adults, anonymous responses).
2. Written approval from the CSO daily supervisor that engineers may take
   part during working time.
3. Informed consent from every participant, covering:
   - participation is voluntary and they may stop at any time;
   - no performance evaluation of the participant takes place;
   - responses are anonymous;
   - the data will be used for the thesis.
4. **No internal data.** Every stimulus is a public CVE (NVD + EPSS + CISA
   KEV), scored by the project's public-data models. No Deutsche Bank asset,
   finding or configuration appears anywhere in the materials.

## 3. Participants

- **Who:** CSO security engineers who triage vulnerability or cloud findings
  as part of their job.
- **Target:** n ≥ 12. That is two complete 6 × 6 Latin squares: 12, 18 or 24
  participants keep the design balanced.
- **Recorded:** role and years of triage experience only, in bands. No names
  or employee IDs.
- **What this sample size can detect** (paired test, Wilcoxon efficiency
  0.955, power 0.8):

  | n | α = 0.05 | α = 0.01 (Holm, worst case over 5 comparisons) |
  |---|---|---|
  | 12 | d_z = 0.91 | d_z = 1.19 |
  | 18 | d_z = 0.72 | d_z = 0.91 |
  | 24 | d_z = 0.61 | d_z = 0.77 |

  At n = 12 only large effects are detectable. A null result at that size
  must be reported as "underpowered", never as "no difference".

## 4. Explanation formats (within-subject)

| id | format | content |
|---|---|---|
| F0 | **Score only (control)** | model score, rank in the month, CVSS, EPSS |
| F1 | **SHAP waterfall** | TreeSHAP (M-XGB): top 8 features, log-odds, raises/lowers |
| F2 | **LIME bar chart** | LIME weights (M-XGB): top 8 features |
| F3 | **Counterfactual "what-if"** | "Would enter this month's top-100 if EPSS rose from x to y" (D3 L1); or "no EPSS value would escalate it" |
| F4 | **EBM term contributions** | Explainable Boosting Machine (D4): the term contributions behind its own score and rank |
| F5 | **Plain-language summary** | fixed template over the top-3 SHAP factors: "Ranked #r of N. Main reason: … (raises the score); then …; then …" |

- Text components are always shown with their top terms, e.g.
  `text_03 [sql, sql injection, injection]`.
- F4 explains a different model (the EBM) from F1–F3 and F5. Its card
  therefore shows the EBM's own score and rank, so that every card explains
  the ranking it shows.

## 5. Stimuli and assignment

- **Pool:** 24 public test CVEs, drawn once (seed 0) from the D2 explanation
  sets, in three strata:
  - 8 inside their month's top-10;
  - 8 ranked 11–500;
  - 8 ranked below 500.
- **Blocks:** six blocks of four CVEs, each block holding a mix of strata.
- **Assignment:** a 6 × 6 Latin square assigns formats to blocks per
  participant group. Every participant sees all 24 CVEs once and every format
  exactly four times, and both order and the CVE-format pairing are
  counterbalanced.
- **Files:**
  - Latin square: `outputs/d6_latin_square.csv`.
  - Stimuli and answer key: `outputs/d6_stimuli.csv` and
    `outputs/d6_stimulus_cards.html`, generated by
    `python -m src.explainability.human_study`.

## 6. Procedure (about 45 minutes)

1. **Consent and briefing** (5 min). The briefing covers what KEV and EPSS
   are, what "the model" is, and that there are no right or wrong opinions.
2. **Training** (5 min). One worked example per format, on CVEs that are not
   in the pool.
3. **24 trials** (about 30 min), in six format blocks. Each trial runs as
   follows:
   - **T1 forward simulation:** "Will the model place this CVE in this
     month's top-100?" (yes / no). The answer key is the model's actual
     decision.
   - **T2 comprehension:** "Which factor contributed most to this score?",
     four options. The key is the top factor of the explanation shown; for F0
     it is the top SHAP group, so F0 measures guessing from score/CVSS/EPSS
     alone.
   - **T3 decision:** "Would you escalate this CVE for patching this month?"
     (yes / no).
   - **T4 trust in this ranking** (1–7).
   - **Decision time:** from card shown to T3 answered, logged automatically.
4. **Block questionnaire, after each format block:**
   - a 6-item short form of the Jian et al. (2000) trust-in-automation scale
     (1–7);
   - one item: "this format helped me understand why" (1–7).
5. **Debrief** (5 min). The participant ranks the six formats by preference
   and adds free-text comments.

## 7. Measures and analysis (fixed now)

**Per participant × format**, averaged over that format's four trials:

- T1 accuracy;
- T2 accuracy;
- median decision time;
- mean T4 trust;
- block trust score;
- "helped me understand" rating.

**Appropriate reliance:**

- T3 agreement with the KEV outcome, separately for KEV and non-KEV CVEs;
- trust calibration: the correlation between T4 and the model's actual
  correctness on that CVE.

**Primary outcome:** T1 forward-simulation accuracy. This is the standard
behavioural test of whether an explanation conveys what the model does
(Doshi-Velez & Kim 2017).

**Tests:**

- **Across the six formats:** Friedman test, then Nemenyi.
- **Each format vs F0:** Wilcoxon signed-rank, Holm-adjusted over the five
  comparisons.
- **Effect sizes:** matched-pairs rank-biserial r, with 95% CI.
- **Everything else:** secondary measures use the same tests, are reported
  as secondary and are not Holm-adjusted across measures. They are labelled
  exploratory.

**Exclusions** (fixed now): a participant is excluded if they do either of
the following, with the count reported:

- fail both training-check questions;
- complete fewer than 18 of 24 trials.

**Reporting:** every outcome is reported whether significant or not, along
with the detectable-effect table above.

## 8. What would count as a negative result

The negative result would be: no format beats F0 on T1 or T2. It would mean
that, for this model and this audience, the explanations do not convey model
behaviour beyond the score itself. That would be a finding for SQ2, not a
failure of the study.

## 9. Threats

- **Small n:** see the power table.
- **Expertise heterogeneity:** recorded in bands, reported descriptively.
- **Novelty effects:** the training phase reduces them.
- **F4 explains a different model:** stated on the card.
- **Text components are only partly interpretable:** shown with their terms,
  and the D1 finding that text adds little is mentioned in the briefing.
- **The model's ranking is itself weak** (PR-AUC around 0.02): participants
  are told this. Good trust calibration means *low* trust in many cards, so
  higher trust is not "better".
